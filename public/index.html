    How a reel is made

    1. IdeaTrends, a topic bank and past results
    2. ScriptClaude writes it beat by beat
    3. ReviewA second pass scores and rewrites it
    4. VoiceWith word timings
    5. ImagesFrom the library or generated
    6. MotionType, layout and animation per beat
    7. RenderSound effects, music, ffmpeg
    8. Self-checkClaude reviews the video and fixes it

    Learn from a reel
    
      
    Upload a reel you like, from any niche. The engine studies its typography, colours, stickers, animations, pacing, sound effects and script shape, and keeps only what suits your channel and looks good (it skips anything odd, overkill or specific to their content) as ingredients. New reels keep their own look and borrow one to three ingredients for the frames where they fit, so your reels stay yours but keep getting fresher. (For a trial you can copy a style's whole format.)

    Motion library
    
        
      
    
      
    Every animation the engine can use, with a short sample clip (library images, sound effects on, no voice). Rate them: Like ones get used where they fit, Never ones are never used, and new motions stay on trial (at most one per reel) until you rate them. The engine picks from here for each reel and avoids repeating what recent reels used.
    
      
    
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    Asset library
    
      
    Cutout images reused across reels. Backgrounds are removed automatically. Real product photos and images from better free AI tools (Gemini, ChatGPT) look far better than the built-in generator, so upload those whenever you can. Tags are how reels find them (e.g. model, olive, overshirt or tshirt, tee).
    
      
    
        
    
          
    Images your reels need 
    
        
    
        
    Make these in the Gemini or ChatGPT app (free) for the best quality: click Copy prompt, paste it there (for host poses, also attach the host reference photo), download the image, and drop it on the card. It's cut out, tagged and used in your reels automatically. "Upgrade" means a low-quality free-AI version is standing in for now.
